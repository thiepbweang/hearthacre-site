Send a deal

Our JV rules, in writing

  1. Agreement first. Both sides sign HA-JV-1 before the address, the seller's details or our buyer list are shared.
  2. The split. 50/50 of the net assignment fee (or net double-close spread), after title and closing costs. Any other split is written into the agreement before marketing starts.
  3. Paid only at closing. The title company pays both sides from the closing statement. No fees up front, no side payments.
  4. The contract holder runs the seller. One point of contact for the seller. The other side never contacts the seller.
  5. No going around each other. 12 months: neither side buys, assigns or sells that property or that buyer's deal without the other.
  6. Only real contracts. We market a deal only with a signed purchase agreement in hand, with the written disclosures the state requires (Texas and Tennessee both have them).
  7. Buyer deposit. The end buyer puts earnest money with the title company at assignment, usually US$3,000 to US$5,000, set in the assignment.
  8. Closes through title. Assignment or double close through a title company the contract names. Nothing closes outside title.
  9. Data stays private. Seller and buyer contact details are used for that deal only, never resold or added to another list.
  10. Honest numbers. Contract price, ARV, repairs and access are shared in writing, and any change is shared the same day.

1Tell us which parcel
  1. Tell us which parcel

    The address if it has one, or the parcel number from your tax bill, plus the acreage if you know it. That is enough for us to pull the county map.

    How to find your parcel number

    It is on your county property tax bill or appraisal notice, usually called the account number, parcel ID or APN. In Harris County it is the 13-digit HCAD account number. You can also search your name or address on the county appraisal district website.

  2. We check what a builder checks

    Road access, water and electric nearby, septic or sewer, flood zone on the FEMA map, wetlands, deed restrictions and back taxes. You do not need to know any of it; we look it up.

  3. Send a few photos if you can

    As you scroll, the picture shows each shot to take.

    • Road frontageStand on the road and get the whole front of the lot.
    • Corner stakes or survey markersClose on any stake, flag or pin you can find.
    • Anything on the landA mobile home, a barn, a fence, dumping: one wide shot each.
    • An old survey or platA phone photo of the paper is fine, if you have one.

    No photos? That is fine for land. Satellite and county maps get us most of the way.

  4. Your written offer arrives by email

    A clear cash number and a vacant land purchase agreement you can read at your own pace and sign online. Say no and that is the end of it.

  5. The title company sorts out the paperwork

    Back taxes, old liens, heirs who need to sign: the title company handles it and pays them from the sale money. Inherited land with several owners is common, and we are used to it.

  6. You get paid at closing

    By check or wire from the title company, on the date you pick. You never pay us a fee.

1Start outside
  1. Tell us about the house

    A few quick questions by form, text or call. There are no wrong answers, and answering does not commit you to anything.

    • Why you are thinking of selling, and by when
    • Who is on the title, and whether everyone agrees to sell
    • Roughly what is owed: mortgage, back taxes or liens
    • Whether it is empty, rented, or you live there
    • What needs fixing that you already know about
    • The number you would be happy with, if you have one
    Answer the questions
  2. Four photos of the outside

    Phone photos are fine. Walk around once. As you scroll, the picture shows exactly how to take each shot.

    • The frontFrom across the street, phone sideways, roof line to driveway.
    • Each side and the backStep back until the whole wall fits. Get the AC unit in the frame.
    • Foundation cornersCrouch low so the brick and the ground meet. Close up on any crack.
    • Driveway, fence and yardFrom the corner of the lot, all three in one shot.
    How to tell us about the roof

    Stand in the yard and photo each side of the roof. Zoom in on the worst spot: curled or missing shingles, patches, sagging.

    Know when it was replaced? A receipt, a permit or a rough year is perfect. Inside, photo any brown stains on ceilings. That tells us about leaks.

  3. The big four: AC, water heater, panel, roof

    Their age shapes the number more than anything else. You do not need to know the years. As you scroll, the picture shows each shot to take.

    • Outside AC unitThe whole unit from a few steps back, then close on the sticker on its side.
    • Water heater and AC labelsFill the frame with each label. The serial number tells us the year.
    • Electrical panelDoor open, all breakers in one shot, then the label inside the door.
    • RoofFrom the yard, each side of the roof. Zoom in on the worst spot.
    How to find your AC and heat type

    Outside unit: the metal box with a fan on top beside the house. There is a sticker (data plate) on its side with the brand, model and serial. Take one clear, close photo of that sticker. The serial usually tells us the year it was made, so you do not need to know it.

    Inside unit: look in a hallway closet, the garage or the attic. If it has a gas pipe and a flue going up, it is a gas furnace. Photo its sticker too.

    Heat pump or not: if the outside sticker says "heat pump", or your thermostat has an "Em Heat" or "Aux" setting, it is a heat pump. Window units or wall units only? Just tell us.

    How to find your water heater

    Usually in the garage, a utility closet or the attic. A tall round tank, or a small box on the wall if it is tankless.

    Gas or electric: a gas one has a pipe with a valve going in near the bottom and a metal flue pipe on top. An electric one has a power cable and no flue.

    Photo the label on the tank. It shows the size in gallons and a serial number that usually tells us the year.

    How to find the electrical panel

    A grey metal box with a door, usually in the garage, a utility room, a hallway or on an outside wall near the meter.

    Open the door and take one photo of the breakers and one of the label inside the door (it shows the brand). If you see screw-in fuses instead of switches, tell us. That matters for the number.

    Sewer or septic: what happens when you flush

    City sewer: your water bill has a sewer or wastewater charge on it. Most homes in town are on sewer.

    Septic tank: no sewer charge on the bill, and somewhere in the yard (often 10 to 20 feet from the house) there are one or two round lids or a concrete cover. Common in the country and on bigger lots.

    Not sure? Look at an old water bill, or the closing papers from when you bought it. You can also call the county or city utility office and ask. Same for water: city water or a well.

  4. Inside: every room, just as it is

    No cleaning, no fixing. We buy as is. As you scroll, the picture shows how each inside photo should look.

    • KitchenFrom the doorway, wide: counters, cabinets, stove and sink.
    • Each bathroomFrom the door: tub or shower, toilet and vanity together.
    • Each bedroomStand in a corner, lights on, blinds open.
    • Living roomOne wide shot from the corner farthest from the window.
    • Floors up closePoint down at the floor. Any damage, stains or soft spots.
    • Stains and cracksClose on any ceiling stain, wall crack, water or mould.

    Also: the porch and front door, any broken or boarded windows, and the garage and attic if they are easy to get to.

    What you signHouse Purchase and Sale Agreement (As Is)

    • Earnest money goes to the title company within 3 days and counts toward the price.

    After you sign, a buyer or an inspector visits once. You choose the day and time. No open houses and no stream of strangers.

    A local title company checks the title, pays off any mortgage or back taxes from the sale, holds the money, and pays you at closing by check or wire. You never pay us a fee, and who pays closing costs is written into the offer before you sign.

Good to know before you start

The questions most sellers ask us, answered plainly.

How we set the price

We compare your house with recent sales nearby, take off what the repairs in your photos will cost, and leave room for the buyer who does them. You see the number in writing before you decide anything.

What it costs you

No commission and no fee to us. Who pays which closing costs is written into the offer before you sign. Your mortgage, back taxes or liens are paid out of the sale money, not out of your pocket.

Behind on payments or taxes

That is common and fine. The title company gets the exact payoff amounts and clears them at closing. If a tax sale or foreclosure date is close, tell us the date first so we can work to it.

Tenants, or you still live there

We buy houses that are empty, rented or lived in. Tell us about any lease. Need time to move? Ask before you sign, and if it works the move-out date goes into the agreement in writing.

Inherited, or more than one owner

Everyone on the title signs, including heirs. If the estate is not settled yet, the title company tells us what papers are needed, and we can wait for them.

Changing your mind

Before you sign, say no at any time and that is the end of it. Once you sign, the agreement binds both sides, so read it first and ask us anything. Our 30-day inspection period is written in it.

What you leave behind

Take what you want and leave the rest. No cleaning out, no hauling.
